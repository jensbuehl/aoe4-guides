What the two markers are doing

Analysis

Short answer: not broken, but the pair is doing two different jobs in one visual voice, and it costs the list its rhythm.

1 · They are different kinds of thing, drawn the same

Advance to Feudal Age is an action — something the player does at a moment, exactly like "build a house". Feudal Age reached is a state change — a boundary the build crosses. Right now both are the same full-width gold bar at the same weight, so the list gives no clue which one you do and which one just happens.

2 · Eight bars compete with the steps

In this build there are 8 marker bars against 11 step rows. The markers are the loudest thing on the page — full width, gold, boxed — while the steps that carry the actual instructions are quieter. The emphasis is inverted.

3 · The transition is invisible

The gap between "Advance to" and "reached" is a real phase with its own rules: landmark under construction, no new villagers from it, vulnerable window. On screen it is just two bars with unrelated-looking rows between them. Nothing says those rows belong to the transition.

4 · The reached-marker has no time

The one number a reader wants from "Feudal Age reached" — when — is not on it. It is inferred from the row above. The bar occupies a full row and carries a word count of three.

5 · The boundary leans on an icon that cannot carry it

The mark on the arrival bar is the game's own age asset (assets/res/age_2.webp) — a circled Roman numeral. It is the correct, canonical icon and there is nothing to swap it for. But at 20px in gold it is a near-twin of a circled transport glyph, and Ⅱ / Ⅲ / Ⅳ differ from each other by one stroke.

So the icon should not be what tells you a boundary was crossed. Let the bar treatment and the age name carry that, and keep the asset as identification rather than as the signal.

6 · Repetition

"Advance to Feudal Age" / "Feudal Age reached" — the word Age four times in eight rows, before the other two ages. The age name alone identifies it.

Recommendation

Option B. Keep both markers, but stop drawing them alike: the advance becomes a step row because it is a step, and only the arrival stays a bar because only it is a boundary. Bracket the rows between them so the transition reads as a phase. This is the smallest change that fixes the semantics, and it halves the bar count without deleting information.

If the appetite is smaller, Option A alone is worth shipping: turning the advance into a step row and putting the arrival time on the bar fixes the two observations that cost a reader the most, and touches one row type.

  • The transition is a phase now, with a duration a reader can compare against their own game. That number is the single most requested thing about a fast castle.

  • Cost: one nesting level in the renderer, and the duration has to be derived. Both are already true for the alternatives block.
